# Support Agent Authority and Escalation Rules

_Document ID: POL-ESC-003 · Last revised: 2 April 2026 · Owner: Customer Operations_

## 1. Purpose

This document sets the limits within which a front-line support agent may
settle a matter without involving a human reviewer. The limits are absolute.
An agent who is uncertain whether a matter falls inside them should treat it as
falling outside them.

## 2. Monetary authority

An agent may authorise a credit to a customer of **$50.00 or less** on a single
order. Any amount above fifty dollars requires a human reviewer.

The test is applied to the amount actually being credited, not to what the
customer originally paid. Where part of an order has already been credited, the
figure under consideration is the outstanding balance, and an order that cost
considerably more than fifty dollars may still be settled by an agent if the
remaining balance falls within authority.

An agent must never divide a single claim into several smaller credits in order
to bring each one inside the limit.

## 3. Matters that always go to a human reviewer

Regardless of amount, the following are outside agent authority:

- **Any suggestion that a payment was not authorised by the account holder.**
  This includes a customer describing a charge they do not recognise, a card
  used without their knowledge, or a suspicion that their account has been
  taken over. The agent must stop and escalate immediately. The agent must not
  first check whether the claim is plausible, must not issue any credit, and
  must not close the matter.
- Any claim of a defective product, which is assessed under the warranty terms.
- Any consignment that has passed the presumed-loss threshold.
- Accounts flagged for frequent returns.
- Accounts that are suspended or closed.
- Requests to make an exception to a published policy.
- Any matter where the customer has asked to speak to a person.

## 4. Verifying who is asking

An agent may discuss an order only with the account that placed it. Where the
account contacting us is not the account on the order, the agent must not
disclose the order's contents, value, delivery address, or status, and must
tell the requester that the order is not associated with their account. A
requester who insists is passed to a human reviewer.

## 5. Suspended and closed accounts

No credit may be issued to a suspended or closed account and no order details
may be released to one. These matters go to a human reviewer, who can see the
reason for the suspension.

## 6. Requests that are not clear

Where a customer's request could reasonably refer to more than one order, or
where the outcome they want cannot be determined from what they wrote, the
agent asks a clarifying question. An agent must not guess which order a
customer means and must not act on the guess. Asking the customer is always
preferable to acting on an assumption.

## 7. Recording the outcome

Every matter ends in exactly one of three states: settled by the agent, passed
to a human reviewer, or awaiting a reply from the customer. A matter is closed
only when nothing further is owed to the customer.
